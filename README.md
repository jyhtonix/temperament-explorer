To access: https://jyhtonix.github.io/temperament-explorer/

## Features

- **20 multiple-choice questions** covering study habits, teamwork, communication, decision-making, stress and problem-solving
- **Four temperament profiles:** Choleric, Sanguine, Phlegmatic and Melancholic
- **Automatic scoring** with ranked results and support for tied scores
- **Progress tracking** with Back and Next navigation
- **Personalised insights:** potential strengths, blind spots and self-reflection questions
- **Mobile-friendly** responsive layout
- **Print or save results as PDF**
- **Fully offline:** no account, API key or server needed

## How Scoring Works

Each question has exactly **four answer options**, with one option mapped to each temperament. The app counts the selected responses and displays the four totals, which add up to **20** when every question is answered.

## The Four Temperaments

| Temperament | Nickname | Key Traits |
|---|---|---|
| **Choleric** | The Driver | Goal-oriented, decisive, assertive |
| **Sanguine** | The Socialiser | Outgoing, enthusiastic, sociable |
| **Phlegmatic** | The Peacemaker | Calm, patient, cooperative |
| **Melancholic** | The Analyst | Analytical, careful, detail-oriented |

### 1. Choleric: The Driver
- Likes taking charge and solving problems
- Focuses on results, efficiency and leadership
- May become impatient when things move too slowly
- Often comfortable making difficult decisions

### 2. Sanguine: The Socialiser
- Enjoys meeting people and sharing ideas
- Often energetic, optimistic and expressive
- May become distracted or struggle with follow-through
- Often enjoys presentations and group activities

### 3. Phlegmatic: The Peacemaker
- Prefers harmony and stable environments
- Often a good listener and dependable team member
- May avoid confrontation or resist sudden changes
- Often helps maintain a peaceful working environment

### 4. Melancholic: The Analyst
- Likes accuracy, structure and careful planning
- Often values quality, logic and high standards
- May overthink decisions or worry about mistakes
- Often excels at detailed analysis and systematic work

> **Note:** These are traditional categories, not definitive scientific classifications. Many people show a mixture of two or more temperaments.

## About Temperament Tests

A temperament test is a type of personality assessment that helps you understand your natural behavioural tendencies, emotional reactions, communication style, and how you interact with other people. It helps answer questions such as:

- Why do I behave this way?
- Why do I react differently from others?
- What kind of environment suits me best?

### What is it used for?

| Area | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Career development** | Understand your preferred working style |
| **Education** | Recognise differences in how students communicate and learn |
| **Leadership** | Improve teamwork, decision-making and conflict management |
| **Relationships** | Understand why people respond differently to the same situation |
| **Self-awareness** | Identify strengths, weaknesses and areas for growth |

## Temperament vs. Other Assessments

A temperament test is not exactly the same as a personality test.

| Assessment | Main Focus |
|---|---|
| **Temperament test** | Natural behavioural tendencies and emotional responses |
| **MBTI** | Preferences in how you perceive information and make decisions |
| **Big Five** | Five broad personality traits, including openness and conscientiousness |
| **DISC** | Behavioural and communication styles, often used in workplaces |

> **Your temperament is not your destiny.** Behaviour can change with experience, environment, maturity and conscious effort.

## Accuracy and Limitations

- The traditional four-temperament model is **not a clinically validated personality assessment**.
- Results are presented as **self-reflection**, not a psychological diagnosis or a definitive personality classification.
- Questions and scoring logic have been checked for internal consistency, but the assessment has **not been psychometrically validated** with a student sample.

## Classroom Tips

- Describe this as a **Temperament Self-Reflection Test**, not a formal psychological test.
- Encourage honest answers over "ideal" answers.
- Use the self-reflection questions as discussion prompts in class.
